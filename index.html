About

Studying how compact objects grow and eject matter

How compact objects grow, what environments they inhabit, and how they eject material are fundamental open questions in astrophysics. My work addresses these questions using radio observations of transient events, supported by multiwavelength data and theoretical modelling.

I received my PhD in 2021 from Monash University for my thesis titled "On the nature of neutron stars in accreting systems". Since then I have been a postdoctoral researcher at ICRAR-Curtin University working on accreting black hole research.

I lead international observing programmes with facilities around the world including the Australia Telescope Compact Array, the Very Large Array, MeerKAT, and the GMRT. I also supervise research students and contribute to university teaching, professional service and public engagement.

My earlier research focused on accreting neutron stars, X-ray binaries and thermonuclear X-ray bursts. This work continues to inform my broader programme on accretion and outflows from compact objects.

Research

The physics behind cosmic explosions

My research connects transient discovery with long-term radio monitoring and physical modelling to reveal how compact objects accrete matter, launch outflows and shape their environments.

01

Tidal disruption events

Watching dormant black holes come to life

When a star passes too close to a massive black hole, tidal forces can tear the star apart. The resulting flare gives us a rare opportunity to watch a previously quiet black hole begin accreting material and launching an outflow in real time.

I use radio observations to measure the energy, velocity and environment of these outflows, and to understand the connection between accretion discs, jets and winds.

02

Radio transients, jets & outflows

Following cosmic explosions over months and years

Black holes are some of the Universe's most efficient particle accelerators. Radio emission traces shocks as material accelerated and ejected collides with its surroundings. I combine observations from major radio facilities with synchrotron modelling to constrain outflow energetics, geometry and evolution.

03

Nuclear transients & QPEs

Probing black holes in galactic nuclei

Quasi-periodic eruptions, repeating partial disruptions and other nuclear transients are revealing unexpected behaviour around low-mass massive black holes. I use radio follow-up to search for ejections and test how these systems accrete.

04

Compact objects & accretion

Connecting neutron stars and black holes

How does matter behaves in extreme gravitational environments? Observations of thermonuclear X-ray bursts provide the rare opportunity to witness emission directly from the surface of a compact object. My work on observing and modelling the surface of neutron stars aims to understand the physical processes that occur in such extreme environments.

01

Radio emission from tidal disruption events

Use multi-epoch radio data to investigate outflows launched when stars are destroyed by massive black holes including illimunating the environment of quiescent black holes and how much energy the outflows carry away.

02

Jets and outflows from cosmic transients: how are accretion flows and outflows coupled across the mass scale?

Incorporating multiwavelength data to constrain and understand when and how outflows and jets are launched by accretion flows.

03

Modelling thermonuclear explosions

Thermonuclear X-ray bursts are the most common thermonuclear explosions in the Universe. Modelling them helps us learn about nuclear reaction pathways we cannot replicated on Earth, as well as how matter behaves at the densities reached in neutron stars.
